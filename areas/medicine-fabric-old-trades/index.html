Medicine, Fabric and Old Trades

This is the Old Quarter that tourists often miss, and locals still use every day. Lan Ong and Thuoc Bac smell of dried herbs and roots from the traditional medicine trade, named after the great physician Hai Thuong Lan Ong. Around them, a working museum of guild streets survives: tin, copper, fabric, hats, bamboo and rattan, each still selling what its name promises. It is the least crowded cluster and the most rewarding for anyone who loves how things are made.

The streets of this area

Numbered in walking order. Tap a street for its full story.

1Traditional medicineLãn Ông

Shops of roots, herbs and remedies named after the master physician Hai Thuong Lan Ong.

2Chinese medicine wholesaleThuốc Bắc

The wholesale medicine street, fragrant with dried herbs stacked to the ceiling.

3FabricHàng Vải

Fabric traders serving tailors and garment makers across the city.

4Tin wareHàng Thiếc

Tinsmiths hammering pots, trays and kitchen ware from sheet metal.

5Copper and bronze wareHàng Đồng

Copper and bronze sellers, from incense burners to kitchen pots.

6Tobacco pipesHàng Điếu

The pipe sellers' street, once supplying every kind of tobacco pipe and accessory.

7Woven bamboo screensHàng Cót

Bamboo weavers whose street now mixes craft shops with fashion and food.

8Poultry tradingHàng Gà

The old poultry street, today a local shopping and food street.

9Bamboo craftHàng Tre

Bamboo and rattan craft sellers keeping a light, patient trade alive.

10Lacquered bamboo bucketsHàng Thùng

Once makers of lacquered bamboo buckets, now mixed local trade.

11Bowls and dishesHàng Bát

The bowl and dish street, today also groceries and household goods.

12Hats and conical hatsHàng Nón

Hat sellers of every shape, from conical palm hats to modern caps.

13Rattan craftHàng Mây

Rattan weavers and sellers of baskets, lamps and furniture.

14Charcoal, now green rice cakesHàng Than

The charcoal street that became known for banh com green rice cakes.
