Eat and Drink in the East

When the Old Quarter gets hungry, it walks east. Ta Hien is the famous bia hoi corner where the city sits on small stools with glasses of fresh beer, and the streets around it layer grill smoke, noodle steam and dessert carts into one long evening. Ma May and Hang Buom keep the older food traditions too, from festival sweets to family restaurants that have served the same dishes for decades.

The streets of this area

Numbered in walking order. Tap a street for its full story.

1Bia hoi cornerTạ Hiện

The backpacker corner of Hanoi: fresh beer, grilled food and stools on the pavement.

2Old food streetMã Mây

A heritage street of preserved shophouses, restaurants and small hotels.

3Woven sails and mats, now foodHàng Buồm

A former guild street of the Chinese community, today a busy food street.

4Fish sauce and dried seafoodHàng Mắm

The fish sauce street, pungent and proud, supplying kitchens across the quarter.

5Salt trading, now snacks and coffeeHàng Muối

The old salt street, today a local mix of snack shops and coffee corners.

6Timber rafts, now food marketHàng Bè

Named for the timber rafts of the Red River trade, now a fresh food market street.

7Tea houses and night snacksĐào Duy Từ

An evening street of desserts, tea and late snacks near the bia hoi corner.
