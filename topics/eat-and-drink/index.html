Eat and drink your way around

Five stops, no long sit down dinners, prices shown before you order. Written by Suzy, in the first person, because that is the only honest way to guide.

Hanoi does not do tasting menus in the Old Quarter. It does one dish, perfected over decades, served on a small stool at the exact hour it tastes best. Eating well here is a route, not a reservation.

These are the five stops I build that route from. Graze them in order and by the last one you will understand why I never plan a long sit down dinner in the quarter.

01

The lunch bowl

Known forBun cha, grilled pork with noodles and herbs
Best timeLate morning to early afternoon

Bun cha is a lunch dish, and it keeps lunch hours. Grilled pork arrives still smoky, the herbs are half the point, and the dipping bowl does the talking. Order once, watch how the table next to you assembles a bite, then copy them without shame.

See the food walk

02

Hang Buom

Known forGrill rows and smoke
Best timeEvening

When the grills light, Hang Buom becomes the quarter's barbecue hall. The rule that protects you here is the rule of the street itself: prices are shown before you order. Point, confirm the number, then sit. Small plates, big smoke, no surprises.

Open Hang Buom

03

The egg coffee pause

Known forEgg coffee, whipped and warm
Best timeMid afternoon, when your feet vote no

Every good route has a seated pause in it. Egg coffee is Hanoi's: a small cup, a whipped top like warm custard, and ten unhurried minutes while the street performs below the balcony. This is not a caffeine stop. It is a reset button.

Read before you go

04

Ta Hien

Known forOne fresh glass on a small stool
Best timeEvening

Bia hoi is brewed daily and priced for everyone, which is why the corner fills with grandparents, students and travellers in the same happy row. One glass is the ritual; a second is the conversation starter. The stool is small. The welcome is not.

Open Ta Hien

05

The night market spine

Known forSnacks the length of Hang Dao
Best timeFriday to Sunday evenings

On weekend nights the traffic leaves and the spine of the quarter becomes a slow river of snacks. Treat it as a tasting corridor: something grilled, something sweet, something you cannot name yet. That last one is usually the one you talk about at home.

See the night market area

Five stops, five small bills, zero regrets. If you want this route walked with you, timed to the grills and the market hours, that is exactly what the food walk was designed for.
