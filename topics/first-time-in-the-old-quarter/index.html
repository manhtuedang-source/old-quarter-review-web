First time in the Old Quarter

Six anchors that turn a beautiful maze into a place you can read. Written by Suzy, in the first person, because that is the only honest way to guide.

Everyone I meet arrives with the same two feelings: delight, and a slight panic about the traffic. Both are correct. The Old Quarter is 49 guild streets packed into about a square kilometre, and it does not explain itself in order.

So do not try to see it all. Take these six anchors in the order I give them, and the maze turns into a map you carry in your head. This is the same route logic I use when friends visit me in Hanoi.

01

Hoan Kiem Lake

Known forThe quarter's compass
Best timeEarly morning, or lit up after dark

Start every day here and end every evening here. The lake is the one fixed point the whole quarter hangs from: south of it the streets calm down, north of it they tangle. Walk it once before dark on arrival day and your bearings are set for the whole trip.

See the lake area

02

Dong Xuan Market

Known forThe wholesale heart
Best timeMorning, before the heat

The great covered market is where the quarter buys from itself. Go in the morning when the lanes are busiest and the fabric, dried goods and kitchenware floors make sense as one living system. You are not here to shop yet; you are here to understand how the streets outside are supplied.

See the market area

03

Hang Ma

Known forFestival colour
Best timeSoft morning light; loudest before Tet and Mid Autumn

If one street explains the Vietnamese calendar, it is this one. Paper offerings, lanterns and decorations change with every festival, so the street you photograph in January is not the street you get in September. Come early for the light, come before a festival for the full volume.

Open Hang Ma

04

Ma May

Known forThe old merchant house
Best timeAfternoon

Ma May keeps one of the quarter's great survivors: a merchant house from another century, arranged around its courtyards exactly as a trading family lived in it. Step inside after the market noise and the whole quarter suddenly has a domestic scale again.

Open Ma May

05

Ta Hien

Known forThe bia hoi corner
Best timeEvening

By day it is a pretty street of flags and old facades. By night it becomes the quarter's living room: small stools, fresh beer drawn the same day, everyone facing the street like it is a stage. Sit once, even for one glass, and you have done the most Hanoian thing there is.

Open Ta Hien

06

The train street

Known forTrains through a neighbourhood
Best timeOnly from behind the barriers, on railway rules

Yes, the trains really pass that close to people's front doors. No, you do not step onto the tracks for a photo. The honest way to see it is from behind the barriers like the residents do, and it loses none of its wonder for being safe.

See the west edge

Six anchors, one square kilometre, and the panic is gone. From here the quarter stops being a maze and starts being a neighbourhood, which is when the real exploring begins.
